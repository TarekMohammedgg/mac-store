# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: the store owner and staff who run inventory day to day in admin — adding devices and accessories, keeping stock and photos accurate, checking stats, and reducing repetitive “is this still available?” questions.

Secondary: bilingual (EN/AR) shoppers browsing the public catalog before visiting — inspecting real photos and condition so they can decide whether a trip is worth it.

Both surfaces share one design system; public and admin are first-class. Brand register is the default when a task doesn’t specify otherwise; admin work still follows the same honest, calm voice.

## Product Purpose

Mac Stock is a private inventory system with a public catalog. Owners manage used Apple devices and accessories; shoppers inspect real stock remotely before they come in person. Success means the owner moves stock quickly and shoppers rarely need to ask whether an item is available or what condition it’s in.

## Positioning

See the actual device before you come — honest used Apple inventory; what you see is what’s available.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: Browse into a device detail; when ready, contact the store or visit in person.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: See the real device before you come — an honest relationship between the store and the customer.
- Belief ladder: (1) This is a real local store with real stock. (2) Photos and condition are honest. (3) The device I want is worth coming to see.
- Proof on hand: Real product photos and condition notes, plus store contact, location, and social links.

## Brand Personality

Honest, calm, precise. The interface should evoke quiet confidence — “I can trust this.” Public and admin should feel like the same careful store, not a loud marketplace bolted onto a dashboard.

## Anti-references

Not loud marketplace clutter (flash badges, neon CTAs, sale noise). Not generic purple SaaS / AI-template dashboards. Not heavy enterprise admin (dense gray chrome with no breathing room).

## Design Principles

1. **Honesty over hype** — Show the product as it is; never decorate trust away with promotional noise.
2. **Quiet confidence** — Restraint, whitespace, and clarity beat visual loudness; calm UI earns belief.
3. **One voice, two jobs** — Public catalog and admin share the same system; admin stays precise without becoming enterprise-heavy.
4. **See before you go** — Every public screen reinforces remote inspection that makes an in-person visit worthwhile.
5. **Ops that protect trust** — Admin speed exists so the catalog stays current; outdated stock breaks the promise.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA. Support full EN/AR localization with correct RTL mirroring. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Maintain strong contrast and visible keyboard focus on both catalog and admin surfaces.
